## Front Doors: The Focal Point

Few things influence a home’s curb appeal like the front door. It’s the one place on your exterior where color can really make a statement—bold or subtle, elegant or playful. Whether you're selling your home or just refreshing its look, painting the front door is one of the simplest ways to make a strong, lasting impression.

![front door color](/images/doors/fd-gloss.jpg)

A carefully chosen front door color sets a welcoming tone, hints at your style, and invites people in before they’ve even crossed the threshold. A deep black door suggests timeless sophistication. A cheery yellow or bold red feels warm and energetic. A slate blue or forest green leans classic and calm. The best part? There’s no “right” answer—just the one that fits *you*.

> Your front door is the first detail people notice as they approach your home. Whether it feels bold, elegant, or neutral sets the tone for everything that follows.

### Harmony with Natural Elements

#### Work with What You Have

Fixed materials like brick, stone, and roofing won't change, so your front door color should complement those tones. Matching warm or cool undertones keeps the overall look cohesive.

#### Tie in the Landscape

The natural environment surrounding your entry—plants, mulch, walkway colors—affects how your door color reads. A door that echoes elements of the landscape feels grounded and intentional.

### The Power of Sheen

#### Why Semi-Gloss Works

A semi-gloss finish offers the perfect balance between elegance and practicality. It reflects light in a way that draws attention, while also standing up to the wear and tear of everyday use.

#### Prep Is Key to a Clean Finish

Higher-sheen paints highlight surface imperfections, so careful sanding and priming are critical. Professional prep ensures the final coat looks smooth, polished, and long-lasting.

### Black Doors and Storm Doors

#### Classic—but Not Always Right

Black is timeless and versatile, but in certain settings, it can look too heavy or flat. Lighter trim or brighter hardware can help lift the look and keep it from disappearing into the facade.

#### How Storm Doors Affect Appearance

Glass storm doors can darken the appearance of your front door and magnify heat retention. Even a bright color can look muddy behind glass if the lighting and angles are unfavorable.

### Dark Colors + Sunlight = Trouble

#### The Problem with Heat Buildup

When a dark-colored door sits in direct sunlight behind a storm door, it can become extremely hot. That heat can damage the paint over time, leading to bubbling, cracking, or premature fading.

#### Lighter Shades, Longer Life

Choosing a lighter or mid-tone color can reduce surface temperature and help the paint last longer. This small adjustment can make a big difference in durability and appearance.

### A Smarter Way to Choose

#### The Cutout Trick

Printing a photo of your house and cutting out the door gives you a quick way to “try on” colors. Holding chips behind the hole helps you eliminate colors that clash and identify those that work.

### Oil Gloss Front Doors

Fine Paints of Europe in Vermont produces their Hollandlac. It is brilliantly bright and sleet-storm durable.

#### A Bold, Durable Choice

Oil-based gloss finishes offer the highest sheen available, creating a rich, mirror-like surface that’s both dramatic and timeless. These finishes also offer the best weather resistance, making them ideal for doors exposed to sun, wind, and rain. Deep colors like navy, red, and black stay vibrant longer and resist fading better than water-based alternatives.

#### More Time, More Cost—But Worth It

Producing an oil gloss finish is a labor-intensive process requiring multiple coats, sanding between layers, and long drying times. It’s not a fast or budget-friendly option—but for homeowners who want the most striking, durable, and luxurious result, it's well worth the investment.

#### Oil Options Aren’t All Equal

There are water-based “oil-look” or gloss latex paints that mimic the appearance of true oil gloss—but they’re not the same. These finishes offer less gloss, lack the deep, mirror-like depth, and are more prone to fading over time. While they’re more affordable and easier to apply, they don’t offer the same durability or long-term impact as a true oil gloss finish.
