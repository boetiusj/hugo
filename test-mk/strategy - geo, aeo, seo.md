## Purpose & Strategic Frame

This plan consolidates Crestwood Painting’s GEO, AEO, and SEO strategy into a single, practical system. The objective is not to publish more content, but to improve clarity, extractability, and trust for both homeowners and answer engines.

The strategy focuses on making Crestwood easier for AI systems to understand, summarize, and confidently recommend.

## Core Principle: Signal Density Over Page Length

Answer engines do not skim. They extract.

High performance comes from fewer vague paragraphs and more explicit answers, evidence, and structure. Each page should clearly communicate:

* Who Crestwood is
* What Crestwood does, precisely
* Why a first‑time visitor should trust Crestwood

Clarity compounds. Volume does not.

## Page Architecture Overview

Recommended structure per service page:

* 600–900 words of focused long‑form copy
* 3–5 embedded Q&A blocks
* 2–3 case snippets
* 1 embedded short video
* Existing panels retained (review widget, service cards, knowledge panels)

This produces dense but readable pages that support both human decision‑making and AI extraction.

## Q&A Blocks Strategy

### What Q&A Blocks Are

Q&A blocks are not traditional FAQs. They are embedded, standalone answers written in natural language and placed contextually within the page.

Each block addresses a real homeowner question and provides a direct, quotable answer that an answer engine can lift without interpretation.

### Placement Guidelines

Q&A blocks should appear:

* After section headers
* After service explanations
* Before pricing or scheduling discussions
* Near common decision friction points

Target 3–5 Q&A blocks per page.

### Example

**Do I need to move furniture before interior painting starts?**

In most cases, no. Our crews handle basic furniture moving and protection as part of the setup. Larger items like pianos or fully packed bookcases may need to be relocated ahead of time, which we’ll discuss during the walkthrough. Projects are planned so homes remain livable throughout the process.

## Experience Signals with Timestamps

### Why They Matter

Answer engines value both longevity and recency. Older dates signal stability and accumulated knowledge. Recent dates signal relevance.

Both should be present.

### Example Experience Signals

* Painting Kansas City homes since 2007
* Over 18 years of continuous local operation
* Exterior systems refined after the 2011–2012 drought summers
* Crew protocols updated post‑COVID (2020)
* Prep standards revised for Midwest humidity (2022)
* Scheduling buffers added after 2023 freeze‑thaw cycles
* Product selection reviewed annually, most recently 2025
* Long‑term repaint performance observed over 9–12 year cycles

These signals should be woven naturally into copy, Q&A answers, and case snippets.

## Comparative Language Strategy

### Purpose

Comparative language clarifies tradeoffs and signals expertise without naming competitors. It helps homeowners understand positioning while remaining factual and calm.

Use sparingly: one or two statements per page.

### Example Comparative Language

* Some painters optimize for speed; we optimize for preparation.
* Fast quotes are easy; accurate scopes take time.
* Not all paint failures show up on day one—we plan for year five.
* We don’t treat occupied homes like empty job sites.
* A smooth schedule matters as much as a smooth wall.
* Paint fails quietly—our prep prevents that.

## Case Snippets Strategy

### What Case Snippets Are

Case snippets are short, structured proof points. They are not testimonials, galleries, or long narratives.

They function as evidence.

### Placement

Case snippets work best:

* Near the end of service pages
* Before primary calls to action
* On city‑specific pages
* Occasionally adjacent to Q&A blocks

### Volume and Dating

* 2–3 snippets per page
* Include light date references
* Older dates remain valuable when framed as durability proof

### Example Format

* Location
* Problem
* What was done
* Outcome

Example:

Brookside home, exterior repaint (Summer 2024)

Weathered siding with prior coating failure. Full scrape, spot prime, and flexible exterior system. Even finish with no callbacks through the following freeze‑thaw season.

## Video Shorts Strategy

### Purpose

Video shorts compress trust. They explain how Crestwood thinks, not just what it sells.

### Format

* 30–60 seconds
* One idea per video
* Talking‑head or jobsite footage
* Calm, instructional tone

### Topics

* Why paint fails
* Prep decisions that matter
* Scheduling realities
* Midwest weather considerations
* Common homeowner misconceptions

### Volume and Placement

* Start with 10–12 total videos
* Approximately one new video per month
* Embed one relevant video per service page
* Maintain a secondary video library page for browsing and sharing

Social platforms such as Instagram can be used for redistribution, but on‑site embedding is the priority.

## Edit vs Add: How Existing Copy Changes

### Guiding Rule

Edit first. Then add.

### What to Trim

* Repetitive service descriptions
* Generic trust statements
* Vague reassurance paragraphs
* Redundant explanations

If a paragraph does not answer a question, prove experience, or guide a decision, it should be reduced or removed.

### What Remains

* Core process explanations
* Homeowner‑centered framing
* Calm, confident tone
* A clear narrative spine

Q&A blocks, case snippets, and video now handle much of the closing work.

## Implementation Priorities & Next Steps

### Phase 1

* Reduce existing service page copy by approximately 15–25%
* Add 3–5 Q&A blocks per page
* Add 2 case snippets per page

### Phase 2

* Record and embed the first 6–8 videos
* Add experience signals with timestamps

### Phase 3

* Extend the structure to city‑specific pages
* Complete remaining videos
* Rotate case snippets seasonally if desired

## Canonical Q&A Block Template

Use this structure consistently across the site.

**Question**
Written exactly how a homeowner would ask it. Include location when natural.

**Answer**
Three to five sentences. Begin with a direct answer. Clarify scope. Reference experience or process. End with reassurance or expectation‑setting.

Avoid bullets, links, and sales language. This block is an answer, not a pitch.
